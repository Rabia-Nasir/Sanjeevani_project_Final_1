3 1. Introduction  
1.1 Purpose  
This Software Requirements Specification (SRS) defines the functional and non -functional requirements 
for CanSupport AI, an AI -powered patient engagement platform being developed for Sanjeevani – Life 
Beyond Cancer, a Mumbai -based NGO supporting cancer pat ients across India. The document is 
intended to guide design, development, testing, and academic evaluation of the system over a three -
month project timeline.  
1.2 Document Conventions  
Functional requirements are labelled FR -<module>.<number> (e.g., FR -3.2) and prioritised as High, 
Medium, or Low. Non -functional requirements are labelled NFR -<number>. "Shall" denotes a mandatory 
requirement; "should" denotes a recommended one.  
1.3 Intended Audience and Reading Suggestions  
This document is intended for the student development team and project guide/evaluator  who may 
review functional scope. Sections 1 –2 provide context; Section 3 is the core functional specification; 
Sections 4 –8 cover interfaces, quality attributes, data, and the emotional -scoring model; Section 9 is a 
supporting appendix.  
1.4 Project Scope  
CanSupport AI will provide a public, login -free patient portal with an AI chatbot capable of answering 
questions about Sanjeevani's programs, hospitals, and volunteers in English and Hindi. Answers are 
grounded in Sanjeevani's  own verified content using Retrieval -Augmented Generation (RAG), preventing 
fabricated information. The system additionally analyses the emotional tone of patient messages to 
adapt its responses and to escalate conversations showing signs of significant d istress to a human 
counsellor. An admin dashboard gives Sanjeevani staff visibility into query volume, response 
performance, and aggregate emotional trends. The system does not replace counsellors, does not 
provide medical diagnosis or treatment advice, and does not perform any clinical psychological 
assessment.  
1.5 Definitions, Acronyms and Abbreviations  
• RAG — Retrieval -Augmented Generation: a technique that grounds LLM output in retrieved source 
documents.  
• NLP — Natural Language Processing.  
• LLM — Large Language Model.  
• EDS — Emotional Distress Score, an experimental support -prioritisation metric defined in Section 8.  
• SLA — Service Level Agreement (here, an internal response -time target).  
• Knowledge Base — the indexed corpus of Sanjeevani's website, program documents, annual 
reports, and hospital/volunteer data.

2. Overall Description  
2.1 Product Perspective  
CanSupport  AI is a new, standalone web platform. It is not a replacement for Sanjeevani's counselling 
staff but a self -service and triage layer in front of them: it answers what it can verify, and routes the rest 
— along with an AI -drafted reply and, where relevant,  an emotional -urgency flag — to a human.  
2.2 Product Functions  
• Browse Sanjeevani programs and find nearby volunteers/counsellors without registering.  
• Ask free -text questions in English or Hindi and receive grounded, verified answers within seconds.  
• Have the system recognise emotional signals (fear, confusion, distress, etc.) and adapt tone and 
content accordingly.  
• Automatically escalate conversations showing sustained or severe distress to a counsellor.  
• Give Sanjeevani staff a dashboard of query volume, response times, and aggregate emotional 
trends.

5. System Architecture Overview  
The end -to-end processing pipeline for a single patient message is as follows:  
• 1. Patient submits a message via the portal chat widget.  
• 2. NLP layer performs language detection, intent classification, and entity extraction (Module 2).  
• 3. Affective computing layer analyses emotional signals and updates the conversation's running 
Emotional Distress Score (Module 4).  
• 4. The query, together with detected intent and emotional context, is used to retrieve relevant 
passages from the knowledge base (Module 3).  
• 5. The LLM generates a response grounded in the retrieved passages, adapted in tone and content 
to the detected emotional state.  
• 6. If retrieval confidence is low, or EDS crosses a distress threshold, the conversation is routed to 
the counsellor escalation queue with an AI -drafted reply (Module 5).  
• 7. The response is returned to the patient; all steps are logged for the audit trail and analytics.  
6. Non -Functional Requirements  
6.1 Performance  
• NFR-1: The chatbot  shall return a response within 5 seconds under normal load for at least 95% of 
queries.  
• NFR-2: The platform shall support at least 200 concurrent chat sessions without degradation, 
scalable via cloud infrastructure.  
6.2 Security and Privacy  
• NFR-3: All data in transit shall be encrypted via HTTPS/TLS; sensitive data at rest shall be encrypted.  
• NFR-4: The patient portal shall not require login or collect identifying information beyond what a 
patient voluntarily provides in a message.  
• NFR-5: Dashboard access shall be role -based and authenticated (see FR -5.5).  
• NFR-6: Personal and health -related information shall be handled in line with India's Digital Personal 
Data Protection Act, 2023.  
6.3 Usability and Accessibility  
• NFR-7: The portal shall be usable by patients with limited digital literacy, using plain -language UI 
copy in both English and Hindi.

counsellor as a priority action, not resolved by AI alone.

Sanjeevani staff.  
7. Data Requirements  
7.1 Knowledge Base Data  
Sourced from Sanjeevani's  website, the 14 program documents, annual reports, and volunteer -hospital 
directory data. Each source document is indexed with metadata (program, hospital, region, last -updated 
date) to support traceability (FR -3.5) and targeted refresh (FR -3.6).

Conversation transcripts, detected intent, detected language, emotion labels, and EDS values are stored 
per conversation to support escalation, analytics, and quality review. Conversations are not linked to 
patient identity unless the patient voluntarily p rovides contact details for follow -up.

8. Emotional Distress Score (EDS) Specification  
The EDS is an experimental, non -clinical metric used solely to help prioritise  counsellor attention. It is 
computed per conversation from four weighted inputs: emotion intensity, negative sentiment, repeated 
distress signals across turns, and the presence of explicit escalation phrases. It is reported to counsellors 
as a prioritisat ion aid only and must always be labelled as such (FR -4.6, NFR -13). 
EDS Range  Category  System Action  
0 – 30 Normal conversation  No action; standard chatbot flow continues.  
31 – 60 Emotional support 
recommended  Chatbot  offers counselling -resource information 
alongside its answer.  
61 – 80 Counsellor attention 
recommended  Conversation is added to the escalation queue for 
review within the standard SLA.  
81 – 100 Priority human review  Conversation is flagged 🔴 for immediate 
counsellor attention; AI continues to respond 
supportively while awaiting human contact.  
9. Appendix  
9.1 Use Case Summary  
• UC-1: A newly diagnosed patient asks a general "what do I do first" question and receives 
programme  guidance plus nearby volunteer contacts.  
• UC-2: A caregiver asks about financial assistance for chemotherapy and receives grounded 
eligibility information or escalation if the knowledge base is inconclusive.  
• UC-3: A patient expresses fear the night before chemotherapy; the system responds with 
simplified, reassuring information and offers counselling resources.  
• UC-4: A patient's messages show escalating distress across a conversation; the system raises EDS, 
continues to respond supportively, and immediately flags the conversation for priority counsellor 
review.  
• UC-5: Sanjeevani management reviews the dashboard to see which programs and hospitals 
generate the most queries, and which stages of the patient journey show elevated anxiety.
